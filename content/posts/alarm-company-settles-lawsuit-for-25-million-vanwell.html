A Real-World Lesson: The Vanwell Electronics Case

Consider this sobering scenario: A hotel clerk working the front desk was in danger of being attacked. She pressed the panic button — her one lifeline in that moment — and nothing happened. No alarm, no response, no help.

The button had been broken for weeks. And according to court records, the alarm company responsible for the system — Vanwell Electronics — had known about the malfunction for approximately 16 weeks without fixing it.

The clerk was severely injured. Vanwell Electronics ultimately settled the lawsuit for $2.5 million. The monitoring company, Criticom Monitoring Services, was dismissed from the case — because it had no contractual obligation to repair panic buttons and never received a signal from the broken device. Three Critical Lessons for Alarm & Security Companies

1. Your Contracts Define Your Liability

The Vanwell case hinged in part on what the company had committed to in its service agreement. The company “installed and maintained the hotel’s security system” — and that word maintenance proved critical. Review every contract carefully. Be deliberate about what obligations you agree to, and work with a legal professional familiar with the alarm industry to ensure your agreements are properly structured.

2. You Are in the Life-Safety Business

Unlike many industries, alarm and security companies deal directly with the protection of human lives and property. A broken panic button isn’t an inconvenience — it can be the difference between life and death. Conduct your business accordingly. Implement internal protocols to track outstanding repair requests, and never let a known life-safety defect go unaddressed.

3. Insurance Is Not Optional — It’s Essential

It appears Vanwell Electronics had insurance coverage — the $2.5 million settlement strongly suggests it. But not every alarm company is adequately covered. A lawsuit of this scale could bankrupt a business without proper protection. General liability alone is often not enough; alarm companies also need errors & omissions (E&O) coverage, completed operations coverage, and more.
