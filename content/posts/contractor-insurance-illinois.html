Workers’ Compensation Insurance in Illinois

If your contracting business has employees, workers’ compensation should be reviewed immediately. The Illinois Workers’ Compensation Commission states that if an employer has one employee, even a part-time employee, the employer must obtain workers’ compensation insurance, with rare exceptions. The IWCC also states that employees are covered from the moment they are hired.

The penalties can be serious. Illinois notes that an employer that knowingly and willfully fails to obtain workers’ compensation insurance may face fines of up to $500 for every day of noncompliance, with a minimum fine of $10,000, and the Commission may issue a work-stop order until proof of insurance is provided.

This is one of the most important areas for Illinois contractors to review, especially if you have crews, use laborers, hire part-time help, work with subcontractors, or are growing from a solo operation into a crew-based business.

Construction Worker Classification in Illinois

Illinois construction businesses should also pay close attention to how workers and subcontractors are classified. The Illinois Department of Labor states that individuals performing services for construction contractors are presumed to be employees unless they meet the criteria in the Employee Classification Act. The Department may assess civil penalties and seek other remedies if a contractor misclassifies employees as independent contractors.

This matters for insurance because worker classification can affect workers’ compensation, general liability audits, payroll reporting, subcontractor certificates, and contract compliance.

Roofing Contractor Insurance in Illinois

Roofing is a specialized trade in Illinois. The Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation lists Roofing Contractor, Licensed as a regulated profession.

The IDFPR roofing contractor application packet states that the RF-INS form is the only accepted proof of liability and property damage insurance. The same form states that roofing contractors must hold property damage insurance of at least $250,000 for each occurrence and liability insurance of at least $500,000 for each occurrence of personal injury or bodily harm.

Roofing contractors with employees may also need to provide proof of workers’ compensation insurance as part of the application process.

Plumbing Contractor Insurance in Illinois

Plumbing is also regulated at the state level. The Illinois Department of Public Health states that individuals who plan, inspect, install, alter, extend, repair, and maintain plumbing systems in Illinois must be licensed. IDPH also states that it licenses plumbers, plumbing contractors, plumbers’ apprentices, irrigation contractors, and retired plumbers.

For plumbing contractors, insurance should be reviewed for general liability, workers’ compensation, commercial auto, tools and equipment, completed operations, and any contract-specific certificate requirements.

Chicago Contractor Insurance Requirements

Illinois contractors working in Chicago may face additional requirements. The City of Chicago states that a general contractor license is required to perform most types of building construction, alteration, repair, and demolition work within the city.

Chicago’s municipal code requires proof of commercial general liability insurance before issuance of a general contractor license. Required per-occurrence limits vary by license class, including $5,000,000 for Class A, $3,000,000 for Class B, $2,000,000 for Class C and Class D, and $1,000,000 for Class E. Chicago also allows a combination of primary and umbrella or excess policies to satisfy minimum limits above $1,000,000.

Before starting a project, it is smart to review the local requirements where the work will be performed, especially in Chicago, Cook County, Aurora, Naperville, Joliet, Rockford, Springfield, Peoria, Champaign, and other Illinois municipalities.

Types of Contractor Insurance We Can Review and Quote

Every contractor has different risks. A general contractor, roofer, electrician, HVAC contractor, plumber, concrete contractor, painter, drywall contractor, excavation contractor, remodeler, landscaper, and handyman business may all need different coverage structures.

General Liability Insurance

General liability insurance is often the foundation of a contractor insurance program. It can help protect your business from third-party claims involving bodily injury, property damage, and certain personal or advertising injury claims.

For Illinois contractors, general liability is often requested by customers, general contractors, landlords, municipalities, property managers, and project owners. It may also be needed for local licensing, permitting, subcontract agreements, commercial leases, and certificate requests.

A policy review can help confirm whether your limits, endorsements, exclusions, and completed operations coverage are appropriate for the work you perform.

Workers’ Compensation Insurance

Workers’ compensation insurance can help cover medical expenses and lost wages for employees injured on the job. For contractors, this is especially important because jobsite injuries can be severe and expensive.

If you have employees, hire laborers, use part-time help, or are adding payroll, your workers’ comp policy should be reviewed for proper classification codes, payroll estimates, officer inclusion or exclusion, subcontractor handling, Illinois coverage, and audit exposure.

This is especially important for out-of-state contractors doing work in Illinois. The Illinois Workers’ Compensation Commission states that if an out-of-state company conducts business with employees in Illinois, that company must provide a workers’ compensation policy that includes Illinois coverage for those workers.

Commercial Auto Insurance

Personal auto insurance is usually not designed for business vehicle use. If you drive trucks, vans, trailers, or work vehicles for your contracting business, commercial auto insurance may be needed.

This coverage can help protect your business if you or an employee is involved in an accident while driving for work. It can also help satisfy job, lease, municipal, or contract requirements.

Contractor Tools and Equipment Insurance

Your tools are your business. If your saws, compressors, ladders, lifts, generators, diagnostic equipment, concrete tools, welding equipment, landscaping equipment, or specialty tools are stolen or damaged, a standard property policy may not be enough.

Contractor tools and equipment coverage, often written as inland marine insurance, can help cover business tools and mobile equipment while they are in transit, at a jobsite, or stored temporarily.

Builder’s Risk and Installation Floater Coverage

If you are responsible for materials, fixtures, equipment, or work in progress before a project is completed, builder’s risk or installation floater coverage may be important.

This can be especially useful for contractors installing cabinets, flooring, HVAC equipment, electrical systems, plumbing materials, windows, doors, roofing materials, fire protection components, fencing, siding, or other project materials.

Professional Liability or Errors & Omissions Insurance

Some contractors provide design-build work, consulting, inspection, project management, technical recommendations, system design, or construction management services. In those situations, general liability may not respond to every claim.

Professional liability or errors and omissions coverage can help protect against certain claims involving mistakes, negligence, design errors, or failure to perform professional services.

Contractor Pollution Liability

Certain trades may have environmental or pollution exposure. This can include excavation, demolition, roofing, HVAC, plumbing, remediation, fuel system work, painting, concrete cutting, sewer work, mold-related work, or jobs involving chemicals, dust, contaminants, or hazardous materials.

Contractor pollution liability should be reviewed if your work could involve cleanup costs, third-party bodily injury, third-party property damage, or environmental claims.

Umbrella or Excess Liability Insurance

Some contracts require higher liability limits than a standard policy provides. Umbrella or excess liability insurance can provide additional limits over underlying policies such as general liability, commercial auto, and employers’ liability.

This can be important if you bid larger jobs, work for commercial clients, sign municipal contracts, work in Chicago, or need $2 million, $5 million, or higher liability limits.

Contractor Bonds

Some contractors need bonds for licensing, permitting, public projects, or contract requirements. A bond is not the same as insurance, but it may still be required before you can start certain work.

We can help review whether you need a contractor license bond, permit bond, bid bond, performance bond, payment bond, or maintenance bond.

FAQ: Contractor Insurance in Illinois

What insurance does a contractor need in Illinois?

Most Illinois contractors should consider general liability insurance, workers’ compensation if they have employees, commercial auto insurance if they use vehicles for work, and tools and equipment coverage. Depending on the trade and job type, they may also need umbrella coverage, builder’s risk, installation floater coverage, professional liability, pollution liability, or contractor bonds.

Is general liability insurance required for contractors in Illinois?

Illinois does not have one single general liability requirement that applies to every contractor in every situation. Requirements depend on the trade, city or county, licensing rules, permit requirements, and contract terms. For example, Chicago requires general contractors to provide proof of commercial general liability insurance, with required limits based on license class.

Do Illinois contractors need workers’ compensation insurance?

Yes, if the contractor has employees, workers’ compensation should be reviewed immediately. Illinois states that if an employer has one employee, even a part-time employee, the employer must obtain workers’ compensation insurance unless a rare exception applies.

Does a sole proprietor contractor in Illinois need insurance?

A sole proprietor with no employees may not need workers’ compensation in the same way an employer with covered employees does, but they may still need general liability, commercial auto, tools coverage, bonds, or proof of insurance to satisfy customers, general contractors, landlords, municipalities, and project owners. A policy review can confirm what coverage fits the business.

Do Illinois roofing contractors need special insurance?

Yes. Illinois roofing contractors are regulated by IDFPR. The IDFPR roofing contractor insurance form states that roofing contractors must hold at least $250,000 in property damage insurance for each occurrence and at least $500,000 in liability insurance for each occurrence of personal injury or bodily harm.

Do plumbing contractors need to be licensed in Illinois?

Yes. The Illinois Department of Public Health states that individuals who plan, inspect, install, alter, extend, repair, and maintain plumbing systems in Illinois must be licensed.

What insurance do Chicago contractors need?

Chicago general contractor insurance requirements depend on license class. Chicago’s municipal code requires proof of commercial general liability insurance with per-occurrence limits ranging from $1,000,000 for Class E to $5,000,000 for Class A. Contractors may also need workers’ compensation, commercial auto, bonds, and other coverage depending on employees, vehicles, and contracts.

What is a certificate of insurance for contractors?

A certificate of insurance, often called a COI, is a document showing proof of insurance coverage. Contractors often need COIs before starting a job, pulling a permit, signing a subcontract, leasing space, or working for a general contractor. A COI does not replace the policy, but it summarizes key coverage details.

Can I add a general contractor or property owner as an additional insured?

In many cases, yes. If your contract requires additional insured status, your policy should be reviewed to confirm whether the proper endorsement is available. Some contracts also require primary and noncontributory wording, waiver of subrogation, completed operations wording, or specific coverage limits.

Why should I review my contractor insurance policy before renewal?

A review can help catch outdated payroll, missing vehicles, incorrect classifications, low limits, subcontractor issues, excluded operations, missing endorsements, and contract gaps. If your business changed during the year, your policy may need to be updated before your next job, certificate request, audit, or renewal.
