Fire suppression insurance, by most standards, will include professional liability, umbrella liability, and protection from on-the-job accidents, which may include workers’ compensation. When it comes to professional liability, numerous risk factors are covered. In the event of damages resulting from on-site erroneous actions, as well as issues with the delivered product or service.

If a worker were to sustain an injury or be required to take medical leave, fire suppression insurance may cover workers’ compensation in the event it is deemed as a result of an on-site incident. 

As for umbrella liability, its coverage is broad as usual, even within the field of fire suppression. When an incident that is not covered by your general insurance policy occurs, umbrella liability can allow for businesses to be further covered and may help to even out the balance when it comes to what your general insurance policy covers.

Fire suppression insurance may cover the following according to prominent experts in the field:

  • Property, including building and equipment

  • Excess/Umbrella liability

  • Auto insurance

  • Workers’ compensation
