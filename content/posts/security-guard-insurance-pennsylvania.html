Pennsylvania Compliance Factors That Influence Insurance

Pennsylvania insurance underwriters and commercial clients often look for documentation and operational clarity that ties directly to PA rules—especially for armed work and licensing.

Act 235: Armed Guard / Lethal Weapons Certification Impacts

If your guards carry a firearm or lethal weapon as part of employment, Pennsylvania’s Act 235 certification requirements can become a major underwriting checkpoint. Carriers may request training records, policy/procedure documentation, and confirmation of armed operations as part of the quote process.

Private Detective Act of 1953: County-Level Licensing Considerations

Pennsylvania’s Business One-Stop Hub notes that security businesses must also comply with the Private Detective Act of 1953, including an application submitted to your County Court (with details in Section 4 of the Act). This county-based framework can create practical differences in documentation and licensing workflow depending on where you operate (e.g., Philadelphia County vs. other counties).

Insurance Requirements for Pennsylvania Security Guard Companies

Insurance requirements for Pennsylvania security companies come from three places:

  1. client contracts (property managers, venues, public-sector bids),
  2. operational exposure (armed vs. unarmed, patrol vs. post), and
  3. payroll and workers’ compensation structure.

Most PA security guard companies commonly carry:

General Liability Insurance (GL)

General liability typically responds to third-party claims such as bodily injury or property damage arising out of your operations. In the security industry, contracts frequently push limits higher than what a small business would carry by default.

Workers’ Compensation Insurance (PA-specific rating environment)

Workers’ comp is usually one of the biggest cost drivers for guard companies, especially with large payroll, high turnover, or frequent contract changes.

Pennsylvania is distinct because PCRB (Pennsylvania Compensation Rating Bureau) plays a central role in the state’s workers’ compensation classification and manual rules, and security operations are addressed in the PA Basic Manual (including the security agency class).

Professional Liability / Errors & Omissions (E&O)

E&O can be important when your contracts or operations create allegations of negligence (failure to prevent an incident, improper reporting, inadequate procedures, etc.). Many buyers require it even for unarmed post coverage.

Commercial Auto (if you patrol or transport guards)

If you operate patrol vehicles, respond to alarms, or transport guards between sites, commercial auto becomes a standard requirement.

Umbrella / Excess Liability

Pennsylvania contracts—especially for larger sites, municipalities, universities, or multi-location property managers—commonly require excess liability limits above the GL and auto policies.

Surety Bonds (when required by contract or county process)

Some agreements or licensing scenarios can require bonds. This isn’t universal, but it’s common enough in the security sector that it should be planned for early.

Contract Insurance Requirements Seen in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, many losses don’t come from “lack of insurance”—they come from certificate and endorsement problems that delay contract starts or cause noncompliance findings.

Common PA contract requirements include:

  • Additional insured endorsements
  • Waiver of subrogation
  • Primary and non-contributory wording
  • Specific certificate holder language
  • Notice of cancellation wording (often requested, sometimes restricted)
  • Higher limits (GL/Auto/Umbrella)

Public-sector and institutional requirements often spell out these terms clearly in their insurance requirement documents.

Cost Factors for Security Guard Insurance in Pennsylvania

There is no flat rate for security guard insurance in PA. Premiums are driven by what you do, where you do it, and how payroll and exposure are documented.

Key factors that affect pricing include:

  • Armed vs. unarmed services (Act 235 armed exposure typically increases underwriting scrutiny)
  • Patrol operations vs. fixed posts
  • Client industries (healthcare, public sector, nightlife, logistics/warehousing, residential)
  • Payroll size and classification accuracy (workers’ comp audit sensitivity)
  • Claims history and loss frequency
  • Vehicle usage and driver controls
  • Required liability limits in contracts

Pennsylvania Workers’ Comp: Audit Pitfalls to Avoid

Workers’ comp audits are where many guard companies get hit with surprise additional premium, especially if:

  • Payroll fluctuates month-to-month,
  • Job duties change by site,
  • Armed work isn’t consistently documented,
  • Subcontractor/1099 usage isn’t handled correctly, or
  • Classifications don’t align with PA manual rules.

Because PA relies on PCRB manual rules and classifications, it’s worth getting the structure correct from day one—before growth accelerates and audits get larger.

Armed vs. Unarmed Security in Pennsylvania

Unarmed operations

Unarmed guard programs tend to have broader carrier options and more flexible underwriting, especially for lower-risk posts and strong training/supervision.

Armed operations (Act 235 considerations)

Armed guard work can require:

  • higher GL and umbrella limits,
  • more restrictive underwriting,
  • written firearms policies and training controls, and
  • clear disclosure of armed exposure.

Act 235 requirements are often referenced in underwriting discussions because the certification is tied to the role and training expectations for armed security in Pennsylvania.

Pennsylvania Security Guard Insurance FAQs

What insurance does a security guard company need in Pennsylvania?

Most Pennsylvania security companies carry general liability and workers’ compensation, and often add E&O, commercial auto, and umbrella based on contract requirements and patrol/armed exposure.

Do armed security guards in Pennsylvania need Act 235?

Armed work in PA commonly ties into Act 235 certification requirements, and underwriters may request documentation of training/certification when armed exposure is present.

Why do Pennsylvania security companies get hit with workers’ comp audit increases?

Audit increases often come from payroll changes, job duty differences by site, classification issues, and incomplete disclosure of armed/patrol exposure, especially as a company grows. PA’s workers’ comp rules and classifications are addressed through PCRB manuals and related guidance.

What contract wording causes the most delays in Pennsylvania?

Additional insured, waiver of subrogation, and primary/non-contributory wording are common pain points, especially for public-sector and institutional buyers that require strict certificate and endorsement language.
