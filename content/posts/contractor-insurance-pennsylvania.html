Pennsylvania Contractors Need More Than a Basic Insurance Policy

Contractor insurance is not one single policy. It is usually a package of coverages designed around your trade, payroll, vehicles, subcontractor use, job size, contract requirements, and risk exposure.

The right coverage can help with:

  • Customer injury or property damage claims
  • Jobsite accidents
  • Employee injuries
  • Stolen tools and equipment
  • Commercial vehicle accidents
  • Subcontractor insurance requirements
  • Additional insured requests
  • Certificates of insurance
  • Contractual insurance requirements
  • Completed operations claims
  • Local permit or registration requirements

Pennsylvania has no statewide license or certification requirement for most construction contractors, but certain contractors are subject to state registration rules, and local municipalities may have their own requirements. The Pennsylvania Department of Labor & Industry notes that most construction contractors are not licensed at the Commonwealth level, while home improvement contractors, crane operators, asbestos contractors, lead contractors, and certain local trades may be subject to separate rules.

That is why a policy review matters. A cheap policy is not always the right policy. You need coverage that matches your work, your contracts, and your Pennsylvania jobsite exposure.

Contractor Insurance Requirements in Pennsylvania

Insurance requirements can vary depending on the type of contractor you are, where you work, whether you have employees, and whether you perform residential or commercial work.

Home Improvement Contractor Insurance in Pennsylvania

Most home improvement contractors who offer or perform home improvement work in Pennsylvania must register with the Pennsylvania Office of Attorney General unless they qualify for an exemption, such as performing less than $5,000 of home improvement work in a calendar year. The PA Attorney General’s FAQ states that covered contractors include self-employed contractors, sole proprietors, subcontractors, independent contractors, corporations, partnerships, and other business entities unless exempt.

For registration, contractors must provide insurance information showing at least $50,000 of personal injury liability coverage and $50,000 of property damage coverage. The PA Home Improvement Contractor registration site also lists proof of commercial general liability insurance with at least $50,000 personal injury coverage and at least $50,000 property damage coverage as required documentation.

Pennsylvania home improvement contractors must also include their PA contractor registration number in advertisements, contracts, estimates, and proposals.

Workers’ Compensation Insurance in Pennsylvania

If your contracting business has employees, workers’ compensation may be required. Pennsylvania’s Department of Labor & Industry states that workers’ compensation coverage is mandatory for any employer with at least one employee who could be injured or develop a work-related disease in Pennsylvania, unless all employees fall into an excluded category.

Pennsylvania municipalities are also required to ask a contractor for proof of workers’ compensation insurance or an affidavit stating that the contractor does not employ others and is not required to carry workers’ comp before issuing a building permit.

This is one of the most important areas to review if you have employees, use subcontractors, or are growing from a solo operation into a crew-based business.

Local Contractor Insurance Requirements

Even if the state does not require a general license for your trade, local cities and municipalities may have their own insurance, licensing, tax, or permit requirements.

For example, the City of Philadelphia lists insurance minimums for certain PA Home Improvement Contractors seeking construction permits, including general liability of $500,000 per occurrence, automobile liability insurance of $300,000, and workers’ compensation limits of $100,000 per accident, $100,000 per employee, and $500,000 policy limit.

Before starting a project, it is smart to review the local requirements where the work will be performed.

Types of Contractor Insurance We Can Review and Quote

Every contractor has different risks. A roofing contractor, electrician, HVAC contractor, concrete contractor, painter, remodeler, and excavation contractor may all need different coverage structures.

At Central Insurance Agency, we can help you review and quote coverage such as:

General Liability Insurance

General liability insurance is often the foundation of a contractor insurance program. It can help protect your business from third-party claims involving bodily injury, property damage, and certain personal or advertising injury claims.

For Pennsylvania contractors, general liability is often requested by customers, general contractors, landlords, municipalities, and project owners. It may also be needed for home improvement contractor registration or local permitting.

A policy review can help confirm whether your limits, endorsements, exclusions, and completed operations coverage are appropriate for the work you perform.

Workers’ Compensation Insurance

Workers’ compensation insurance can help cover medical expenses and lost wages for employees injured on the job. For contractors, this is especially important because jobsite injuries can be severe and expensive.

If you have employees, hire laborers, or are adding payroll, your workers’ comp policy should be reviewed for proper classification codes, payroll estimates, officer exclusions, subcontractor handling, and audit exposure.

Commercial Auto Insurance

Personal auto insurance is usually not designed for business vehicle use. If you drive trucks, vans, trailers, or work vehicles for your contracting business, commercial auto insurance may be needed.

This coverage can help protect your business if you or an employee is involved in an accident while driving for work. It can also help satisfy job, lease, or municipal requirements.

Contractor Tools and Equipment Insurance

Your tools are your business. If your saws, compressors, ladders, generators, diagnostic equipment, or specialty tools are stolen or damaged, a standard property policy may not be enough.

Contractor tools and equipment coverage, often written as inland marine insurance, can help cover business tools and mobile equipment while they are in transit, at a jobsite, or stored temporarily.

Builder’s Risk and Installation Floater Coverage

If you are responsible for materials, fixtures, equipment, or work in progress before a project is completed, builder’s risk or installation floater coverage may be important.

This can be especially useful for contractors installing cabinets, flooring, HVAC equipment, electrical systems, plumbing materials, windows, doors, roofing materials, or other project components.

Umbrella or Excess Liability Insurance

Some contracts require higher liability limits than a standard policy provides. Umbrella or excess liability insurance can provide additional limits over underlying policies such as general liability, commercial auto, or employers’ liability.

This can be important if you bid on larger jobs, work for commercial clients, or sign contracts requiring $2 million, $5 million, or higher limits.

Contractor Bonds

Some contractors need bonds for licensing, permitting, public projects, or contract requirements. A bond is not the same as insurance, but it may still be required before you can start certain work.

We can help review whether you need a contractor license bond, permit bond, bid bond, performance bond, or payment bond.

FAQ: Contractor Insurance in Pennsylvania

What insurance does a contractor need in Pennsylvania?

Most Pennsylvania contractors should consider general liability insurance, workers’ compensation if they have employees, commercial auto insurance if they use vehicles for work, and tools and equipment coverage. Depending on the job, they may also need umbrella coverage, builder’s risk, installation floater coverage, professional liability, pollution liability, or contractor bonds.

Is general liability insurance required for contractors in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania does not have a single statewide general liability requirement for every contractor. However, most home improvement contractors must register with the PA Attorney General and provide insurance information showing at least $50,000 of personal injury liability coverage and $50,000 of property damage coverage, unless exempt. Local municipalities and contracts may require higher limits.

Do Pennsylvania home improvement contractors need to register?

Yes, most contractors who offer or perform home improvement work in Pennsylvania must register with the Pennsylvania Office of Attorney General unless they qualify for an exemption, such as performing less than $5,000 of home improvement work in a calendar year.

Do contractors in Pennsylvania need workers’ compensation insurance?

Pennsylvania workers’ compensation coverage is mandatory for employers with at least one employee who could be injured or develop a work-related disease in Pennsylvania, unless all employees are excluded under the law. Municipalities must also require proof of workers’ compensation or an affidavit before issuing a building permit to a contractor.

Does a sole proprietor contractor in Pennsylvania need insurance?

A sole proprietor with no employees may not need workers’ compensation in the same way an employer with covered employees does, but they may still need general liability, tools coverage, commercial auto, or proof of insurance to satisfy customers, general contractors, landlords, or municipalities. A policy review can confirm what coverage fits the business.

How much contractor insurance do I need in Pennsylvania?

The amount depends on your trade, job size, contract requirements, payroll, vehicles, subcontractor use, and local rules. Many project owners require limits higher than the state minimums for home improvement registration. A policy review can compare your current limits against your actual work and certificate requirements.

What is a certificate of insurance for contractors?

A certificate of insurance, often called a COI, is a document showing proof of your insurance coverage. Contractors often need COIs before starting a job, pulling a permit, signing a subcontract, leasing space, or working for a general contractor. A COI does not replace the policy, but it summarizes key coverage details.

Can I add a general contractor as an additional insured?

In many cases, yes. If your contract requires additional insured status, your insurance agent can review your policy and request the proper endorsement if available. You should never assume additional insured wording is included automatically. Some contracts also require primary and noncontributory wording or a waiver of subrogation.

Why should I review my contractor insurance policy before renewal?

A review can help catch outdated payroll, missing vehicles, incorrect classifications, low limits, subcontractor issues, excluded operations, or missing endorsements. If your business changed during the year, your policy may need to be updated before your next job, certificate request, audit, or renewal.
