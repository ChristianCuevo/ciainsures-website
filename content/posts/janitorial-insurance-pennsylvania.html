Why Janitorial Businesses Need More Than Basic Insurance

Janitorial insurance is not just one policy. It is usually a package of coverages designed around your cleaning services, employees, vehicles, equipment, contracts, locations served, and client requirements.

Business insurance helps protect companies from unexpected costs such as accidents, lawsuits, and other disruptions that could threaten operations. The U.S. Small Business Administration recommends assessing risk, working with a licensed agent, comparing terms and pricing, and reassessing coverage as the business grows.

For a Pennsylvania janitorial company, the right insurance program may help with claims or requirements involving:

  • Slip-and-fall injuries
  • Damage to customer property
  • Cleaning chemical exposure
  • Employee injuries
  • Stolen equipment or supplies
  • Lost customer keys or access cards
  • Employee theft allegations
  • Commercial auto accidents
  • Contract insurance requirements
  • Certificates of insurance
  • Additional insured requests
  • Janitorial bonds
  • Building access and after-hours cleaning risks

Janitorial businesses often work after hours when buildings are empty. Your employees may have keys, alarm codes, badge access, or unsupervised access to client property. That creates a different risk profile than many other small businesses.

Pennsylvania Janitorial Business Requirements to Know

Pennsylvania’s Business One-Stop Hub states that there is no general business license that every Pennsylvania business must obtain. However, it also states that residential and commercial building cleaning services are subject to Pennsylvania sales tax, and cleaning businesses need a sales tax license to collect and remit sales tax on cleaning services.

That does not mean insurance is optional from a practical standpoint. Even when a statewide license does not require a specific insurance policy, commercial clients, landlords, property managers, general contractors, municipalities, and facility owners may still require proof of insurance before you can start work.

Workers’ Compensation in Pennsylvania

If your janitorial business has employees, workers’ compensation should be reviewed immediately. Pennsylvania’s Department of Labor & Industry states that workers’ compensation coverage is mandatory for any employer that employs at least one employee who could be injured or develop a work-related disease in Pennsylvania, unless all employees fall under an excluded category. The state also notes that employers may face lawsuits, criminal prosecution, imprisonment, and substantial fines for noncompliance.

This is especially important for cleaning companies because employees may be exposed to lifting, repetitive motion, ladders, wet floors, chemicals, trash handling, and after-hours building work.

Janitorial Work Has Real Jobsite Exposure

Janitors and building cleaners commonly clean floors, restrooms, windows, walls, frequently touched surfaces, and spills; they may also use equipment such as floor buffers, sprayers, and other cleaning tools. The Bureau of Labor Statistics notes that janitors often spend much of the workday walking, standing, bending, lifting supplies, and moving equipment, and that sprains, strains, pain, and soreness can occur from heavy lifting and repetitive motion.

Cleaning chemicals are another major concern. NIOSH notes that some cleaning chemicals can cause health problems ranging from skin rashes and burns to coughing and asthma, and OSHA lists cleaning industry hazards that include bloodborne pathogens, chemical hazards, confined spaces, electricity, ergonomics, falls, and personal protective equipment.

A good insurance review should consider both client-facing liability and employee safety exposure.

Coverage Options for Janitorial Insurance in Pennsylvania

Every cleaning company is different. A solo residential cleaner may need a different policy than a commercial janitorial contractor cleaning medical offices, schools, warehouses, or multi-location retail stores.

Central Insurance Agency can help review and quote coverage such as:

General Liability Insurance

General liability insurance is often the foundation of janitorial insurance. It can help protect your cleaning business from third-party claims involving bodily injury, property damage, and certain personal or advertising injury claims.

For example, general liability may help if:

  • A visitor slips on a freshly mopped floor.
  • An employee accidentally damages a client’s desk, wall, fixture, or flooring.
  • A cleaning crew knocks over equipment while working in an office.
  • A customer claims your business caused property damage during a cleaning job.

Many commercial cleaning contracts require general liability insurance before work begins. A policy review can help confirm whether your limits, endorsements, and business description match the work you actually perform.

Workers’ Compensation Insurance

Workers’ compensation can help cover medical expenses and lost wages for covered employees injured on the job. For janitorial businesses, this may include injuries from lifting, slips and falls, repetitive motion, equipment use, or exposure to cleaning products.

If you have employees, your workers’ compensation policy should be reviewed for proper classification, payroll estimates, officer inclusion or exclusion, subcontractor handling, and audit exposure.

Business Owner’s Policy

A business owner’s policy, often called a BOP, may combine general liability, commercial property, and business interruption coverage for eligible small businesses. The NAIC explains that business interruption coverage is commonly bundled inside a BOP that also includes business property and liability coverage.

For a janitorial business, a BOP may help protect business property such as office equipment, supplies, cleaning equipment, computers, and certain income interruption exposures after a covered loss.

Commercial Property Insurance

Commercial property insurance can help protect your business property, such as cleaning supplies, office equipment, inventory, storage space contents, furniture, computers, and owned equipment kept at your business location.

This can be especially important if you lease office or storage space for your cleaning business.

Tools and Equipment Coverage

Janitorial companies often rely on equipment that moves from job to job. Vacuums, floor buffers, carpet extractors, polishers, pressure washers, carts, ladders, cleaning machines, and specialty equipment may not be fully protected by a basic property policy once they leave your premises.

Tools and equipment coverage, often written as inland marine insurance, can help protect mobile business equipment while it is in transit, at a client location, or stored temporarily away from your main business location.

Janitorial Bond / Fidelity Bond

Many clients want to know they are protected if an employee steals from them. A janitorial bond or fidelity bond may help reimburse a client if an employee commits a covered theft while working at the client’s location.

This is a major trust-builder for cleaning businesses because your team may have access to offices, desks, storage rooms, inventory, electronics, keys, and secure areas.

A policy review can help determine whether your current coverage includes employee theft, third-party crime, or bonding protection.

Lost Key Coverage

Cleaning companies often hold keys, access cards, key fobs, alarm codes, and building entry credentials. If a key is lost, a client may demand rekeying, lock replacement, or access system changes.

Lost key coverage can be valuable for janitorial businesses that clean offices, apartment buildings, schools, churches, retail locations, or medical facilities.

Commercial Auto Insurance

If your business owns vehicles, uses vans, or sends employees between cleaning sites, commercial auto insurance should be reviewed. Personal auto policies may not properly cover business vehicle use.

Commercial auto coverage can help protect your business from covered auto liability claims and may be required by certain contracts.

Hired and Non-Owned Auto Coverage

If employees use personal vehicles for company errands, supply runs, or travel between job locations, hired and non-owned auto coverage should be considered. This coverage can help protect the business if it is named in a claim involving a vehicle it does not own but uses for business purposes.

Umbrella or Excess Liability Insurance

Some cleaning contracts require higher liability limits than a standard policy provides. Umbrella or excess liability insurance can provide additional limits over qualifying underlying policies.

This may be important if you clean larger commercial facilities, medical offices, schools, government buildings, apartment complexes, or multi-location businesses.

Employment Practices Liability Insurance

Janitorial companies with employees may face employment-related claims involving discrimination, harassment, wrongful termination, retaliation, or wage-related allegations. Employment practices liability insurance, often called EPLI, can help protect against certain covered employment claims.

Pollution or Chemical Liability Coverage

Some cleaning operations involve stronger chemicals, disinfectants, floor stripping products, solvents, or specialty cleaning products. If your work includes industrial cleaning, medical facility cleaning, post-construction cleaning, mold-related cleaning, floor stripping, or disinfection services, chemical and pollution exposures should be reviewed.

FAQ: Janitorial Insurance in Pennsylvania

What insurance does a janitorial business need in Pennsylvania?

A janitorial business in Pennsylvania commonly needs general liability insurance, workers’ compensation if it has employees, commercial auto insurance if vehicles are used for business, tools and equipment coverage, and a janitorial bond or employee dishonesty coverage. Depending on the business, a cleaning company may also need a business owner’s policy, umbrella insurance, lost key coverage, pollution liability, or employment practices liability insurance.

Is janitorial insurance required in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania does not list a universal statewide janitorial insurance policy required for every cleaning business. However, workers’ compensation is mandatory for most Pennsylvania employers with at least one employee, unless all employees are excluded under the law. Many clients, landlords, property managers, and commercial contracts also require cleaning companies to carry liability insurance before work begins.

Yes, if the business has covered employees. Pennsylvania requires employers to insure workers’ compensation liability if they employ at least one employee who could be injured or develop a work-related disease in Pennsylvania, unless all employees are excluded under the law.

What does general liability insurance cover for a cleaning business?

General liability insurance can help cover third-party bodily injury, third-party property damage, medical expenses, legal defense costs, and certain settlements or judgments. For a janitorial business, this may apply to claims involving slip-and-fall accidents, damaged client property, or injuries related to cleaning operations.

What is a janitorial bond?

A janitorial bond, often structured as a fidelity bond, may help protect clients if an employee of the cleaning company commits covered theft while working at the client’s premises. Many commercial clients prefer or require bonded janitorial companies because cleaning crews often work after hours and may have access to private areas.

Does janitorial insurance cover lost keys?

Lost key coverage may be available, but it is not automatically included in every policy. This coverage can help with costs related to replacing keys, rekeying locks, or changing access systems after a covered key loss. Cleaning companies that hold client keys, fobs, badges, or alarm codes should review this exposure.

Do cleaning companies need commercial auto insurance?

A cleaning company should review commercial auto insurance if it owns vehicles, operates vans, transports equipment, or sends employees between job locations. If employees use personal vehicles for work errands or jobsite travel, hired and non-owned auto coverage may also be important.

How much janitorial insurance do I need?
