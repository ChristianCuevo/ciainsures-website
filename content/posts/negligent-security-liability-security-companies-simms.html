Negligent Security Liability: What Security Companies Need to Know

As demand for security services continues to grow, so does the legal exposure facing security companies and property owners. One of the most significant risks in the industry is negligent security liability—a claim that a business failed to provide adequate protection against foreseeable criminal activity.

For security firms, a single negligent security lawsuit can result in substantial financial losses, reputational damage, and lost contracts. Understanding these risks—and having the right insurance protection—is essential for long-term stability.

What Is Negligent Security?

Negligent security claims arise when a business or property owner is accused of failing to take reasonable steps to prevent criminal activity. These cases typically focus on whether:

  • Criminal activity was foreseeable
  • Adequate security measures were in place
  • Industry standards for protection were followed
  • Prior incidents indicated a need for increased security

Security companies may be named directly in lawsuits if their services are found to be inadequate or improperly executed.

The Most Common Negligent Security Claims

Industry data shows that negligent security lawsuits most often involve violent crimes:

  • Assault and battery – 42%
  • Sexual assault or rape – 26%
  • Wrongful death – 15%
  • Robbery – 9%
  • False imprisonment – 4%
  • Other incidents (arson, home invasion, carjacking) – 4%

These claims often involve severe injuries or emotional trauma, which can lead to high settlement amounts and legal costs.

Real Case Example: Prime Hospitality Corp. v. Simms (1997)

A landmark negligent security case highlights how liability can arise even when a criminal act is committed by a third party.

In Prime Hospitality Corp. v. Simms, a hotel guest was attacked, robbed, and sexually assaulted after encountering a suspicious individual near her room. Evidence presented during the case showed that:

  • 56 crimes had occurred on the property in the previous two and a half years
  • Security experts testified the hotel was on notice of ongoing criminal activity
  • The property allegedly failed to implement adequate security measures

The jury found the hotel negligent and awarded $400,000 in damages for pain, suffering, and related losses.

The key legal issue was foreseeability—because prior crimes had occurred, the risk of a serious incident was considered predictable, creating a duty to strengthen security.

Why This Matters for Security Companies

Security firms may face liability if:

  • Guard staffing levels are inadequate
  • Patrols are not performed as required
  • Incident documentation is incomplete
  • Training standards are insufficient
  • Contracted security services do not match actual conditions

Even when a property owner is the primary defendant, security companies are often included in litigation if their services are questioned.

Insurance Coverage for Negligent Security Risks

Because negligent security claims often involve violent incidents, standard policies may not be enough. Security companies should review their coverage for:

General Liability Insurance

Provides protection against third-party injury and property damage claims, including legal defense costs.

Assault and Battery Coverage

Many insurers exclude or limit assault-related claims unless specifically endorsed. This coverage is critical for security operations.

Professional Liability (Errors & Omissions)

Protects against claims related to failure to perform contracted security services properly.

Workers’ Compensation

Covers employee injuries resulting from confrontations or dangerous situations on the job.

Reducing Negligent Security Exposure

Insurance is only part of the solution. Strong risk management practices can reduce both incidents and liability:

  • Conduct site risk assessments
  • Maintain detailed incident reports
  • Verify guard training and certifications
  • Document patrols and response activity
  • Adjust security levels based on crime trends

Security companies can also review national crime data through the Bureau of Justice Statistics to better understand risk patterns.

Demonstrating proactive risk management not only reduces claims but also improves insurability and contract opportunities.
