Why Electricians Need Specialized General Liability Coverage

General liability insurance is designed to protect your business against third-party claims for bodily injury and property damage. For electricians, those risks are significant and specific.

Electrical work creates fire risk, shock hazard, and equipment damage exposure — not just during the job, but months or years after the project is completed. A standard GL policy written for a low-risk service business often doesn’t reflect those realities. Electricians need a policy written with their trade in mind, with underwriters who understand electrical contracting and price the risk accordingly.

Electrical Contractor Insurance Requirements

There are a few things any electrical contractor insurance program should have, as they are things that come up in every job:

Third-Party Bodily Injury

If a homeowner, property manager, or bystander is injured as a result of your work — or while you’re on the job — your GL policy needs to respond. This includes scenarios like a client tripping over your equipment or an injury caused by exposed wiring during an active job.

Property Damage During Operations

Electricians regularly work inside finished structures. Wall cuts, ceiling access, and work near existing systems all create exposure for damaging a client’s property. GL covers you if your operations cause damage to the structure or contents you’re working in or around.

Completed Operations Coverage

This is one of the most critical — and most overlooked — parts of a general liability policy for electricians. Completed operations covers claims that arise after the job is finished. If a fire starts six months later and is traced back to your work, or a panel installation fails and causes equipment damage, completed operations coverage is what responds.

For electricians, this exposure can be severe. Fires and equipment failures tied to electrical work are expensive claims, and the gap between project completion and discovery of a problem can be years. Make sure your policy doesn’t cut off completed operations coverage prematurely or exclude it for certain work types.

Products Liability

If you supply, install, or recommend specific electrical components and one of those products causes a loss, products liability coverage may be relevant. This is especially important for electricians who source their own materials or operate under supply-and-install contracts.

Personal and Advertising Injury

This is a standard component of most GL policies, covering claims like defamation or copyright infringement tied to your business activities. Less trade-specific, but still part of a complete policy.

What’s Often Missing or Inadequate in Electrician GL Policies

Insufficient Completed Operations Limits or Exclusions

Some policies write completed operations at lower limits than the per-occurrence limit, or exclude it for certain project types entirely. For electricians, this is a serious gap. Make sure completed operations is included and written at adequate limits — many contracts require $2M aggregate or higher.

Exclusions for Specific Work Types

Certain electrical scopes — high-voltage work, work on solar or EV charging systems, generator installation — may be excluded by some carriers or require specific endorsements. If your work includes newer technologies or specialized systems, verify your policy doesn’t have silent exclusions that leave you exposed.

No Coverage for Subcontracted Work

If you use subcontractors and one of them causes a loss on your job, the claim may still come back to you as the responsible contractor. Your GL policy should address how subcontracted work is handled and whether your policy responds to losses caused by subs working under your license or contract.

Missing Additional Insured Endorsements

Most contracts — from general contractors, property owners, and municipalities — will require you to name them as an additional insured on your policy. If your policy doesn’t easily accommodate additional insured endorsements, or if your insurer is slow to issue certificates with proper wording, you can lose jobs or create compliance issues on active ones.

General Liability Limits for Electricians: What’s Standard and What Contracts Require

Most electricians carry a base GL policy with limits of $1 million per occurrence / $2 million aggregate. This is the industry standard and the minimum required by most GC and commercial contracts.

However, depending on the work you take on, you may need higher limits or umbrella coverage:

  • Commercial projects often require $2M per occurrence or higher
  • Municipal and government contracts may require $5M or more in total coverage
  • Large GC requirements may specify umbrella limits tied to project value

If you’re bidding commercial, industrial, or government work, review insurance requirements carefully before assuming your current limits are sufficient. Losing a bid because your limits don’t qualify is one of the most preventable problems in contractor insurance.

How Workers’ Comp Interacts With GL for Electricians

General liability does not cover your own employees. If one of your electricians is injured on the job, that’s a workers’ compensation claim — not a GL claim. Electricians often have workers’ comp classified under specific payroll codes that carry higher rates due to the elevated risk of the trade.

Common workers’ comp issues for electricians include:

  • Misclassified payroll — workers doing higher-risk electrical work classified under lower-risk codes, which creates audit exposure
  • Audit surprises — if your payroll grew during the year, expect an audit bill at the end of the policy term
  • Sub exposure — if you use uninsured subcontractors, your workers’ comp carrier may add their payroll to your audit

Keeping clean documentation and verifying sub certificates throughout the year significantly reduces audit volatility.

Do Electricians Need Professional Liability (E&O)?

Standard GL does not cover professional services — meaning decisions, recommendations, or design work you provide as part of a project. For most electricians doing installation work, GL and workers’ comp are sufficient.

However, if you are involved in:

  • Design-build electrical projects
  • Energy consulting or load calculation services
  • Spec recommendations tied to performance outcomes
  • Construction management or coordination roles

…then professional liability (errors and omissions) may be worth considering. As electrical contractors take on more complex scopes — particularly in commercial, solar, and EV infrastructure — the line between installation and professional service blurs.

What Impacts the Cost of General Liability Insurance for Electricians

GL pricing for electricians is driven by several factors:

  • Type of work — residential vs. commercial vs. industrial, new construction vs. service calls
  • Revenue and payroll — larger operations carry more exposure
  • Claims history — prior losses, especially fire or property damage claims, affect pricing significantly
  • States of operation — some states have higher regulatory costs and carrier restrictions
  • Use of subcontractors — and whether you collect and verify their certificates
  • Completed operations history — carriers look at how long you’ve been in business and whether prior completed work has generated claims

A clean loss history and well-documented operations typically result in more competitive pricing and broader coverage options.

Frequently Asked Questions

What general liability limits do electricians typically need? Most start at $1M/$2M, but commercial and government contracts often require higher limits. Review contract requirements before assuming your current coverage qualifies.

Does general liability cover electrical fires caused by my work? If the fire occurs during your operations, GL may respond to third-party property damage claims. If the fire occurs after the job is complete, that falls under completed operations coverage — which must be included in your policy.

Does GL cover my tools and equipment? No. General liability covers third-party claims. Tools and equipment are covered under a separate inland marine or tools and equipment policy.

Do I need GL if I’m a solo electrician with no employees? Yes. GL protects you against third-party claims regardless of whether you have employees. Most clients and GCs will require proof of GL coverage before allowing you on a job.
