Do Alarm Companies Need Errors and Omissions Insurance?

Yes, many alarm companies should strongly consider Errors and Omissions insurance because faulty installation claims are often not limited to physical property damage.

General liability insurance may help when your work causes bodily injury or property damage. But many alarm installer claims involve an allegation that your company made a professional mistake.

That can include:

  • Improper system design
  • Incorrect alarm placement
  • Programming errors
  • Failure to properly test a system
  • Failure to identify coverage gaps
  • Incorrect monitoring setup
  • Delayed or missed service work
  • Failure to meet contract specifications
  • Alleged negligence after a break-in, fire, or security incident

These are the types of situations where professional liability for security installers or E&O insurance may become critical.

For example, if an installer damages a wall while running cable, that may be a general liability issue. But if a client claims the alarm system failed because it was improperly designed, installed, programmed, or serviced, that may become an E&O issue.

That difference matters.

General Liability vs. E&O Insurance for Alarm Companies

Alarm companies often need both forms of coverage because they respond to different types of claims.

The key point is simple: general liability insurance and E&O insurance are not the same thing. Alarm contractors that rely only on general liability may have a major coverage gap when a claim is based on alleged professional negligence, faulty installation, or failure of the system to perform as expected.

Does Alarm Company Insurance Cover Faulty Installation Claims?

It depends on the policy.

Faulty installation claims may involve several different coverage issues. Some claims are tied to physical damage. Others are tied to professional negligence, contract requirements, financial loss, or an alleged failure of the alarm or security system to perform.

That is why alarm companies should not assume that a basic contractor policy automatically covers every installation-related claim.

Faulty installation claims can include allegations such as:

  • The system was installed incorrectly
  • Sensors or cameras were placed in the wrong locations
  • The system was not programmed properly
  • The installer failed to test the system
  • The wrong equipment was recommended
  • A monitoring signal was not set up correctly
  • The system did not meet the client’s written requirements
  • A security or fire event occurred and the client blamed the installer

Some of these claims may fall under general liability. Others may require E&O or professional liability coverage. Some may be limited or excluded depending on the policy wording.

Common Faulty Installation Claim Scenarios

1. Alarm System Fails After a Break-In

A commercial customer experiences a burglary and claims the alarm system did not trigger properly. The customer alleges that the system was programmed incorrectly or that a door contact was not installed where it should have been.

This may create an E&O exposure because the claim is based on the quality of your professional work, not just physical damage caused during installation.

2. Camera System Does Not Capture the Incident

A client discovers that a CCTV system did not record usable footage during a theft, workplace incident, or property damage event. The client claims your company recommended the wrong equipment, installed the cameras incorrectly, or failed to explain system limitations.

This may involve professional liability concerns, especially if the claim alleges financial loss due to your advice, design, or installation decisions.

3. Fire Alarm Component Is Installed Incorrectly

A fire alarm system is installed or serviced, but a component is later alleged to have been installed improperly. If the client, building owner, or another party claims the mistake contributed to property damage or business interruption, the claim may involve both liability and professional negligence questions.

4. Access Control System Creates a Security Gap

An access control system is installed at a commercial facility, school, healthcare building, warehouse, or office property. Later, the customer claims the system allowed unauthorized access because of a setup, programming, or integration error.

This type of claim may not be treated the same as ordinary property damage. The right policy structure matters.

5. Contract Requires Coverage Your Policy Does Not Include

Many alarm and security installation businesses sign contracts that require specific insurance limits, additional insured status, waiver of subrogation, primary and noncontributory wording, or professional liability coverage.

A major issue occurs when the company signs the contract first and checks the insurance later.

FAQ: Alarm Company Insurance and Faulty Installation Claims

Do alarm companies need errors and omissions insurance?

Many alarm companies should consider errors and omissions insurance because general liability may not fully address claims involving professional mistakes, faulty installation, system design errors, programming issues, or failure to perform. E&O insurance is especially important for companies that install, service, monitor, inspect, or design security systems.

What insurance does an alarm company need?

An alarm company commonly needs general liability, errors and omissions insurance, workers’ compensation, commercial auto, tools and equipment coverage, and possibly excess liability or cyber liability. The right package depends on whether the company performs residential work, commercial work, fire alarm installation, monitoring, CCTV, access control, or security system integration.

Does alarm company insurance cover faulty installation claims?

Faulty installation claims may be covered depending on the policy, the type of claim, and the policy wording. Property damage caused during installation may involve general liability, while claims alleging improper design, programming, testing, or professional negligence may require E&O or professional liability coverage.

Is E&O insurance the same as general liability insurance?

No. General liability usually addresses third-party bodily injury and property damage claims. E&O insurance addresses claims involving professional mistakes, negligence, errors, omissions, faulty work, or failure to perform professional services. Alarm companies often need both.

Why is professional liability important for security installers?

Professional liability is important because security installers are often blamed when a system fails to perform as expected. If a customer alleges that your design, installation, programming, testing, or service work caused a financial loss, professional liability or E&O insurance may help respond.

What are common insurance requirements for security system installation businesses?

Common requirements include general liability, professional liability, workers’ compensation, commercial auto, excess liability, additional insured status, waiver of subrogation, and specific liability limits. Requirements often depend on state licensing rules, customer contracts, general contractor agreements, and project type.

Can alarm installers get coverage for fire alarm work?

Some alarm installers can obtain coverage for fire alarm work, but the policy must be reviewed carefully. Fire alarm installation, inspection, testing, monitoring, and service can create different underwriting concerns than basic burglar alarm or CCTV work.

Should an alarm company review insurance before signing a contract?

Yes. Alarm companies should review insurance requirements before signing a contract. Some contracts require specific limits, E&O coverage, additional insured wording, waiver of subrogation, or other endorsements that may not automatically be included in the company’s current policy.
