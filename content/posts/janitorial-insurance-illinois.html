Janitorial Insurance and Business Considerations in Illinois

Insurance needs can vary based on your business structure, number of employees, services performed, locations served, vehicles, client contracts, and local requirements.

Workers’ Compensation Insurance in Illinois

If your janitorial company has employees, review workers’ compensation immediately.

The Illinois Department of Insurance states that if an employer has one employee, even a part-time employee, the employer must provide workers’ compensation insurance coverage. The same source explains that employers can face fines, work-stop orders, and other penalties for failing to provide required workers’ compensation coverage.

This matters for cleaning businesses because employees may mop floors, lift supplies, move equipment, empty trash, clean restrooms, handle chemicals, operate floor machines, and travel between locations. A workers’ comp review can help confirm payroll, classifications, officer inclusion or exclusion, subcontractor handling, and audit exposure.

Illinois Business Registration, Local Rules, and Tax Considerations

Illinois cleaning companies should also review business registration and local requirements. The Illinois Department of Revenue states that businesses must register with IDOR if they conduct business in Illinois or with Illinois customers, with some exceptions. Illinois also offers state resources for registrations, licenses, and permits, and local city or county rules may still apply depending on where your cleaning business operates.

Tax rules can also vary by the type of cleaning service. IDOR states that Illinois generally does not tax sales of service, but Illinois does impose service occupation tax on tangible personal property transferred as an incident to sales of service. IDOR’s audit manual specifically discusses carpet and upholstery cleaning under the Service Occupation Tax Act and addresses how chemicals may create tax treatment issues.

Those rules do not replace insurance requirements. They simply show why cleaning businesses should review both business compliance and insurance before bidding larger jobs or expanding services.

Client Contract Requirements

Many janitorial insurance requirements come from contracts, not the state.

A commercial property manager, school, medical office, general contractor, landlord, retail chain, warehouse, or office building may require:

  • General liability insurance

  • Janitorial bond or employee dishonesty coverage

  • Additional insured status
  • Primary and noncontributory wording

Types of Janitorial Insurance We Can Review and Quote

Every cleaning business operates differently. A solo residential cleaner may need different coverage than a commercial janitorial company cleaning schools, medical offices, warehouses, high-rise buildings, or multi-location retail stores.

At Central Insurance Agency, we can help Illinois cleaning companies review and quote coverage such as:

General Liability Insurance

General liability insurance often forms the foundation of a janitorial insurance program. The Insurance Information Institute describes commercial general liability coverage as protection against financial loss when a business faces liability for property damage, bodily injury, or personal and advertising injury caused by services, operations, or employees.

For an Illinois cleaning company, general liability can help with claims involving:

A visitor slipping on a wet floor

An employee damaging a client’s desk, wall, floor, or fixture

A crew knocking over equipment inside an office

A customer claiming your business caused property damage during a cleaning job

A commercial contract requiring proof of liability coverage

A policy review can help confirm your limits, endorsements, exclusions, and business description.

Workers’ Compensation Insurance

Workers’ compensation can help cover medical expenses and lost wages for covered employees injured on the job. Janitorial workers face risks from lifting, repetitive motion, chemicals, wet floors, trash handling, ladders, and cleaning equipment.

If you have employees, review your workers’ compensation policy for:

Correct classification codes

Accurate payroll estimates

Officer inclusion or exclusion

Part-time employee payroll

Subcontractor handling

Multi-state exposure

Audit preparation

Commercial Auto Insurance

If your cleaning business owns vehicles, operates vans, transports supplies, or sends employees between job locations, commercial auto insurance deserves a close review.

Personal auto insurance may not properly cover business vehicle use. Commercial auto can help protect your business if a covered vehicle accident occurs while your team drives for work. Contracts may also require commercial auto limits before your crew can access a property.

Hired and Non-Owned Auto Coverage

Many cleaning companies do not own every vehicle their workers use. Employees may drive personal vehicles to client sites, pick up supplies, or travel between cleaning locations.

Hired and non-owned auto coverage can help protect the business when a claim names the company after an accident involving a vehicle the business does not own.

Janitorial Bond or Fidelity Bond

Clients often ask whether a cleaning company is “bonded.” A janitorial bond or fidelity bond may help protect a client if an employee steals from the client while working at the premises.

This coverage can help build trust because cleaning crews often work after hours and may access offices, desks, storage rooms, electronics, inventory, keys, alarm codes, and secure areas.

A policy review can help determine whether your current coverage includes employee theft, third-party crime, or bonding protection.

Lost Key Coverage

Cleaning companies often carry client keys, fobs, access cards, badges, and alarm credentials. If a worker loses a key or access device, the client may demand rekeying, lock replacement, or access system changes.

Lost key coverage can help address this exposure when available. Illinois cleaning companies that serve office buildings, apartment buildings, schools, churches, medical buildings, and retail locations should review this carefully.

Tools and Equipment Coverage

Your cleaning equipment keeps your business running. That may include vacuums, floor buffers, carpet extractors, polishers, sprayers, carts, ladders, pressure washers, and specialty cleaning machines.

Tools and equipment coverage, often written as inland marine insurance, can help protect mobile business equipment while it travels between jobs, sits at a client location, or stays temporarily away from your main business location.

Business Owner’s Policy

A business owner’s policy, or BOP, may package general liability, commercial property, and business interruption coverage for eligible small businesses. The NAIC notes that business interruption coverage often comes bundled inside a businessowner’s policy that includes business property and liability coverage.

For a janitorial business, a BOP may help protect office property, computers, supplies, stored equipment, and certain income interruption exposures after a covered loss.

Commercial Property Insurance

Commercial property insurance can help protect your office, storage space, supplies, inventory, furniture, computers, and owned business property at your location.

This matters if you lease a shop, warehouse space, office, or storage unit for cleaning equipment and supplies.

Umbrella or Excess Liability Insurance

Some commercial contracts require higher limits than a standard policy provides. Umbrella or excess liability insurance can provide additional limits over qualifying underlying policies such as general liability, commercial auto, and employers liability.

This coverage can help when your janitorial company bids on larger offices, schools, medical buildings, industrial properties, government contracts, apartment complexes, or multi-location accounts.

Employment Practices Liability Insurance

Cleaning companies with employees can face employment-related claims involving discrimination, harassment, wrongful termination, retaliation, or other workplace allegations.

Employment practices liability insurance, often called EPLI, can help protect against certain covered employment claims.

Pollution or Chemical Liability Coverage

Some cleaning operations involve stronger chemicals, disinfectants, floor stripping products, solvents, degreasers, or specialty cleaning products. Pollution or chemical liability coverage should be reviewed if your company performs floor stripping, industrial cleaning, medical facility cleaning, post-construction cleaning, mold-related cleaning, or disinfection services.

OSHA’s cleaning industry resources specifically identify cleaning chemicals and slips, trips, and falls as important hazard areas for cleaning employers and workers.

    FAQ: Janitorial Insurance in Illinois

    What insurance does a janitorial business need in Illinois?

    A janitorial business in Illinois commonly needs general liability insurance, workers’ compensation if it has employees, commercial auto insurance if it uses vehicles for business, tools and equipment coverage, and a janitorial bond or employee dishonesty coverage. Depending on the business, a cleaning company may also need a business owner’s policy, umbrella insurance, lost key coverage, pollution liability, hired and non-owned auto coverage, or employment practices liability insurance.

    Is janitorial insurance required in Illinois?

    Illinois does not have one universal janitorial insurance package that applies to every cleaning business. However, Illinois requires employers with one employee, even a part-time employee, to provide workers’ compensation coverage unless an exception applies. Clients, landlords, property managers, and commercial contracts may also require liability insurance, bonding, commercial auto, or umbrella coverage before work begins.

    Do cleaning businesses in Illinois need workers’ compensation?

    Yes, if the business has covered employees. Illinois requires workers’ compensation coverage when an employer has one employee, even a part-time employee, with rare exceptions. Cleaning companies should review workers’ comp coverage when they hire employees, add payroll, use part-time workers, or expand into larger contracts.

    Do Illinois cleaning companies need to register with the Illinois Department of Revenue?

    Many businesses must register with IDOR if they conduct business in Illinois or with Illinois customers. Cleaning companies should review IDOR registration, local city or county rules, and tax requirements with the appropriate professional.

    Are cleaning services taxable in Illinois?

    Illinois generally does not tax sales of service, but it does impose service occupation tax on tangible personal property transferred as an incident to a sale of service. Cleaning companies that perform carpet cleaning, upholstery cleaning, scotchgarding, deodorizing, or services involving transferred chemicals should review the rules carefully with a tax professional.

    What does general liability insurance cover for a cleaning business?

    General liability insurance can help protect a cleaning business from third-party bodily injury, third-party property damage, and certain personal or advertising injury claims. For janitorial companies, this may include slip-and-fall claims, damaged client property, or injury claims related to cleaning operations.

    What is a janitorial bond?

    A janitorial bond, often structured as a fidelity bond, may help protect a client if an employee of the cleaning company commits covered theft while working at the client’s location. Many commercial clients prefer or require bonded cleaning companies because crews often work after hours and may access private offices, keys, inventory, and secure areas.

    Does janitorial insurance cover lost keys?

    Lost key coverage may be available, but every policy does not include it automatically. This coverage can help with costs related to replacing keys, rekeying locks, or changing access systems after a covered key loss. Cleaning companies that hold client keys, fobs, badges, or alarm codes should review this exposure.

    Do cleaning companies need commercial auto insurance?

    A cleaning company should review commercial auto insurance if it owns vehicles, operates vans, transports equipment, or sends employees between job locations. If employees use personal vehicles for work errands, supply runs, or travel between cleaning sites, hired and non-owned auto coverage may also matter.

    How much janitorial insurance does an Illinois cleaning business need?

    The amount depends on your contracts, client requirements, payroll, employee count, vehicles, equipment values, cleaning services, claims history, and locations served. A small residential cleaner may need different limits than a commercial cleaning company serving schools, medical offices, warehouses, or multi-location retail accounts.
