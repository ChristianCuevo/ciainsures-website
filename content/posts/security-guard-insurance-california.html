Insurance Requirements for Security Guard Companies in California

Security guard companies in California are typically required to carry multiple lines of insurance depending on services performed, client type, and contractual obligations.

Common insurance requirements include:

  • General liability insurance
  • Workers’ compensation insurance
  • Professional liability / errors and omissions (E&O)
  • Commercial auto insurance for patrol vehicles
  • Umbrella or excess liability coverage
  • Surety bonds, when required by contracts or municipalities

In California, security companies must also comply with licensing and regulatory requirements enforced by the California Bureau of Security and Investigative Services (BSIS), in addition to meeting client and contract-driven insurance standards.

Cost Factors for Security Guard Insurance in California

The cost of security guard insurance in California varies widely. There is no flat rate, and pricing depends heavily on operational risk, payroll structure, and compliance factors.

Key cost drivers include:

  • Armed versus unarmed guard services
  • Patrol operations versus static posts
  • Payroll size and classification accuracy
  • Client industries (tech campuses, healthcare, residential, nightlife, government)
  • Claims history and loss frequency
  • Vehicle usage
  • Contractual liability limits required

California’s strict labor environment and high claim severity mean that improperly structured insurance programs often result in higher premiums or unexpected audit charges.

Workers’ Comp For Security Guards in California

Workers’ compensation is mandatory for security guard companies in California and is one of the most closely scrutinized policies by carriers and regulators.

Common workers’ comp challenges include:

  • Incorrect guard classification codes
  • Armed exposure not properly disclosed
  • Overtime and payroll reporting discrepancies
  • Use of temporary or subcontracted guards

Workers’ compensation guidance and enforcement in California is overseen by the California Department of Industrial Relations (DIR), making accurate classification and reporting critical.

Armed vs Unarmed Security in California

Armed security operations in California face heightened regulatory scrutiny and significantly higher insurance exposure.

Insurance considerations for armed security include:

  • Higher general liability and umbrella limits
  • Additional underwriting review
  • Firearms training and permit documentation
  • Limited carrier availability

Even companies that primarily provide unarmed services must ensure any armed work is properly disclosed to avoid coverage gaps or denied claims.

Common Contract Insurance Requirements in California

Security contracts in California frequently include insurance requirements that exceed standard policy limits. Failure to comply can delay contract awards or disqualify bids entirely.

Common contract requirements include:

  • Additional insured endorsements
  • Waiver of subrogation language
  • Primary and non-contributory wording
  • Elevated liability limits
  • Specific certificate formatting

Proper policy structure ensures certificates can be issued quickly and accurately when contracts are awarded.

What insurance does a security guard company need in California?

Most California security guard companies require general liability, workers’ compensation, and often professional liability, commercial auto, and umbrella coverage, depending on services and contracts.

Costs vary based on payroll, armed versus unarmed services, patrol operations, claims history, and classification accuracy. There is no flat rate.

Do armed security guards require different insurance in California?

Yes. Armed security operations typically require higher liability limits, additional underwriting review, and proof of firearms training and permits.

Is workers’ compensation required for security guards in California?

Yes. Workers’ compensation is mandatory in California, and proper classification is critical to avoiding audits and penalties.
