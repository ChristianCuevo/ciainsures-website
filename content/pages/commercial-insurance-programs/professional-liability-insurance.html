Professional Liability Insurance

Protect Your Business with Professional Liability (E&O) Insurance

Professional liability insurance—also called errors and omissions (E&O) insurance—helps protect your business if you’re accused of a mistake, oversight, or failure to deliver services as expected. These claims often involve financial loss rather than bodily injury or property damage.

In many industries, professional liability coverage is also required to:

  • Qualify for contracts, bids, and RFPs
  • Meet vendor onboarding requirements
  • Satisfy client certificate of insurance standards

If you provide specialized services, guidance, installation work, or professional judgment, professional liability insurance can be a critical part of your risk strategy.

Why Professional Liability Claims Happen

Even strong operators can face allegations such as:

  • Errors, omissions, or negligence in professional services
  • Failure to meet contract scope or performance standards
  • Inaccurate advice, reporting, or documentation
  • Project delays tied to professional services
  • Disputes over deliverables, recommendations, or service quality

These situations can trigger legal expenses and settlements that impact cash flow and growth.

What’s the difference between general liability and professional liability?

General liability typically covers third-party bodily injury and property damage. Professional liability (E&O) typically covers financial harm tied to professional services, advice, designs, or service performance.

Is professional liability the same as errors and omissions insurance?

Yes—E&O insurance is commonly used as another name for professional liability insurance.

Do contracts really require professional liability coverage?

Often, yes. Many clients include professional liability limits and certificate requirements in service agreements, RFPs, and vendor onboarding.

What limit should I carry?

It depends on your industry, contract requirements, the size of projects, and your risk profile. The safest approach is to align limits with your most common (and most demanding) contracts.

Related Coverage Options

Professional liability (E&O) is often just one piece of a complete insurance program—especially for contract-driven businesses. Depending on your operations, you may also want to review these coverages:

  • General Liability Insurance – Helps protect against third-party bodily injury, property damage, and advertising injury claims.
  • Workers’ Compensation Insurance – Provides benefits for employee work-related injuries and illnesses, and helps you stay compliant.
  • Commercial Auto Insurance – Covers vehicles used for business operations, including job-site travel and service calls.
  • Excess / Umbrella Liability Insurance – Adds additional liability limits above underlying policies to help meet larger contract requirements.
  • Cyber Liability Insurance – Helps address data breaches, ransomware, and privacy exposures—especially for businesses handling client data or using connected systems.
